Every statement is tagged with its source: **[owner]** provided by the repository owner (domain knowledge, treated as correct unless data contradict it),
**[doc]** from unofficial documents/AI-generated summaries (approximate, unverified), **[data]** derived from the data here (empirical, may change),
**[assumption]** a working assumption that needs confirmation.

## 3. Plants that matter for interpretation
* **Ilarionas is out of service** [owner] (a fault being repaired). [data] last normal day 12 June 2026; since then zero generation on 118 days and 8 short runs, none above 372 MWh a day (see METHODS §7).
* **February 2026 was exceptionally wet** [owner]: after several years without comparable rain, the dams were opened and water ran almost continuously because the reservoirs could not hold it, while new water kept arriving from the mountains. It is rare but it happened and may recur with climate change. The very high generation days of that month (up to ≈ 48 GWh a day for the 15 large units) belong to this regime and are **not an artefact**; part of that generation was probably forced by the need to release water rather than demand-driven (an inference to test, see `QUESTIONS.md`).
* **Ilarionas capacity:** 412 hm³ in the 2020 presentation [doc]; ADMIE's own total fill rate is reproduced best with ≈ 305 hm³ [data]. Recorded as a hypothesis (412 may be a total, ≈ 305 the useful volume); the switch is left to the December recalibration (K09, D58).
* **Kastraki above 100 %** is a common occurrence [owner]. It is not an anomaly.
* **Amfilochia pumped storage** (planned start ≈ end-2026) will use the Kastraki lake as its lower reservoir [owner]. The owner's estimate is that first filling of its upper reservoirs needs about 9 % of the lake (≈ 4.8 hm³); the recent >100 % levels may be chance or preparation [owner]. [data] single-day swings ≥ 4.8 hm³ are rare (7 of 279 days up to 16 Oct); the strongest 11-day rise of 2026 was +24.6 hm³ (22 Sep → 3 Oct).
* **The Inachos river flows into Kastraki** [owner] — a source of natural inflow not passing through Kremasta.
* **Sfikia and Thesavros** are the two pumped-storage plants whose pumping appears in SCADA (total only) and ISP (per plant).

## 4. ENTSO-E
* The owner believed an ENTSO-E "week" is a weekly average [owner]. [data] confirmed with a precise form: the mean of the daily end-of-day readings, Monday+1 … Monday+7 (see METHODS §2.1). An earlier conclusion in the project history ("end of week") was wrong and was corrected.
* The weekly series lags about one week. Week 34 (17 Aug) is missing from the ENTSO-E series.
* An unofficial document claimed ADMIE values the Aoos water at ≈ 1,795 MWh/hm³ using an internal table. The claim could not be verified and the data do not support it (METHODS §2.1).

## 5. Heads and efficiencies (`data/plant_heads.json`) [doc]
Two PDF/AI-generated tables supplied by the owner give gross head, efficiency range and, for four plants, net head: Kremasta 160 m (net 140–150), Kastraki 95 (80–85), Polyfyto 112 (95–102), Thesavros 172 (150–160), Sfikia 54, Asomata 50, Ilarionas 118, Stratos I 38, Stratos II 6, Platanovrysi 85, Pigai Aoou 670, Pournari I 72, Pournari II 12, Plastiras 577, Ladonas 225.
Five of them were checked against the ENTSO-E data and agree within ≈ 5 % (Kremasta, Kastraki, Polyfyto, Thesavros, Plastiras); the rest are small or not testable. Net heads for plants without a published net head are taken as gross/1.125 (the average ratio of the four that have both).

Source cross-checks of the Sfikia and Thesavros heads are in `SOURCES.md`: the Sfikia value is consistent with the public reservoir levels; the Thesavros gross head was 172 m (the dam height) and was corrected on 17 Oct 2026 to 142 m (net 136–140) from published levels (KNOWN_ISSUES K19, D59); `GrossToNetRatio` is now an explicit parameter so that the correction moved no other plant.

## 7. Glossary
**ISP** integrated scheduling process (schedule) · **SCADA** actual measured output · **RESMV** RES injections at medium voltage · **MCP** market clearing price (day-ahead) ·
**PSH** pumped-storage hydro · **apparent inflow** natural inflow minus unmeasured losses/withdrawals · **river-fed (mixed) PSH** upper reservoir receives natural inflow, **closed-loop PSH** does not · **hm³** million cubic metres · **κ** common scale factor used to compare heads.

## 8. ADMIE history (diagnose run of 17 Oct 2026) [data]
* **The history exists, for both categories, from 2016 to 2026**: daily `.xls` files of `ReservoirFillingRate` and `SystemRealizationSCADA`, covering 91–100 % of the days of every year (the weakest: reservoir files of 2020, 334 of 366 days).
* **API:** `getOperationMarketFilewRange` accepts windows of up to (at least) 366 days; `getOperationMarketFile` answers 200 with an **empty list** for any window longer than one day (so an empty list means "not supported", never "no data").
* **Reservoir files (formats diagnose of 18 Oct 2026):** the old files do not lack reservoirs, they use other labels: `STRATOS1` (now `STRATOS`) and `POURNARI1`/`POURNARI2` (now `POYRNARI1`/`POYRNARI2`, with Y). **Ilarionas is genuinely absent from the 2016 file** (it appears in the 2018 file at 28.6 %; the first date is to be found by the backfill: `Quality.ReservoirFirstDay`). The values are fractions as today (0.808 = 80.8 %), Thesavros appears as `THESAVROS1/2/3` with equal values, and Agras/Edessaios rows exist but are empty.
* **SCADA files:** all 16 units match **exactly** in every year with plausible values (Kremasta 3.77 GWh on 15 Jul 2016). The total and pumping rows were **renamed**, not absent: `ΣΥΝΟΛΟ ΥΔΡΟΗΛΕΚΤΡΙΚΩΝ` (now `TOTAL HYDRO`) and `ΑΝΤΛΗΣΗ` (now `TOTAL PUMPING`) in the same row position (6 for pumping); *that the old pumping row is the same quantity is an assumption*. The sum of the 16 units is 15.9 GWh against a total of 16.4 GWh on 15 Jul 2016, the difference being the small hydro plants that are not among the 16.
* **Duplicates:** in the 2018 reservoir listing 7 days have versions `_01`, `_02` (once `_03`), all published at the same minute (a bulk upload of 13 Mar 2020); in the 2020 SCADA listing 124 days have two records of the **same file path** with different descriptions and publication times, so there is nothing to choose there. Whether the reservoir versions differ in content is recorded by the backfill (`Quality.Conflicts`).
* **Full backfill 2016–2025 (runs of 19 Oct 2026, ≈ 7 minutes a year, no failed download).** Days with reservoir data per year: 360, 351, 362, 364, 334, 361, 363, 365, 366, 365 (2016 → 2025); days without SCADA data: 2 in 2017 and 1 in 2018, none otherwise. **The label changes:** SCADA `TOTAL HYDRO`/`TOTAL PUMPING` from **24 Jul 2020** (before: `ΣΥΝΟΛΟ ΥΔΡΟΗΛΕΚΤΡΙΚΩΝ`/`ΑΝΤΛΗΣΗ`); reservoir names `STRATOS`/`POYRNARI` from **late March 2022** (before: `STRATOS1`/`POURNARI`).
* **Problems found in the data and how they are handled (D83–D85):** (1) **empty cells** (NaN) in 4 yearly files — the first version of the backfill wrote them as `NaN`, which is not valid JSON; now null and `allow_nan=False`; (2) **versions of one day's file that are EMPTY**: on 24 Apr 2018 version `_01` has values and the later `_02` is empty, so "latest" was the wrong rule — the **most complete** version is kept; (3) **files without any value** (2017-04-25, 2018-04-25, 2020-12-14/15, 2021-04-06): invalid, not stored; (4) **days filled with zeros** (13 Jul 2017 and **19–22 Mar 2020**, all reservoirs 0.0 %), invalid; (5) **14 isolated zeros** of one reservoir between normal days (e.g. Kremasta 37.1 → 0 → 36.6 % on 13 Sep 2017): null, listed in `Quality.ZeroGlitches`. The SCADA data are clean: the sum of the 20 units equals TOTAL HYDRO within 2 % on **every** day of the ten years.
* **Independent validation against ENTSO-E (after the repair of 20 Oct 2026):** the stored energy rebuilt from the historical ADMIE fill percentages with the factors calibrated **only on 2026** matches the published ENTSO-E weekly series with an RMS of **0.4–2.0 % of the stock for every year 2018–2025** (389 weeks, RMS **22.3 GWh**, mean error −0.1 GWh, largest 73 GWh; the same reconstruction on 2026 gives 0.6 %). Before the repair the RMS was 69.8 GWh and 2020 had a week 1,308 GWh off: the zero-filled 19–22 March 2020, now invalid. 2025 shows a small positive drift (+29 GWh, +2 %). So the reference capacities of ADMIE did not change over the decade, and the old files are read correctly.
* **Days with a total but no reservoir value:** 25 Apr 2017, 25 Apr 2018, 14–15 Dec 2020, 6 Apr 2021. Their "official total" is not credible: on 15 Dec 2020 and 6 Apr 2021 it repeats the previous day's value (44.45 %, 76.11 %), and 25 Apr 2017 shows 83.13 % between neighbours at 65.4 % — identical to 25 Apr 2018, so that file is probably the 2018 one filed under the wrong date (it also explains a spurious "Ilarionas on 25 Apr 2017"). These days are therefore **invalid together with their total** (D87).
* **Version conflicts of 2018 (5 days: 24 Apr, 9 and 15 May, 8 Jul, 21 Sep):** in each, one version lacks values (Asomata, Ilarionas, Kastraki, … are present in one file and blank in the other); the most complete version is kept (D83).
* **Ilarionas:** the first valid reading is **26 Apr 2018** (42.3 %); before that the reservoir is not published (the 25 Apr 2017 file is empty); after it, 45 single days are missing up to 2025.
* **Pumping (ADMIE system pumping, daily totals).** *Observation [data]:* the old `ΑΝΤΛΗΣΗ` row shows **no step** where it is renamed (mean daily pumping 74 MWh in the 30 days before 24 Jul 2020, 107 MWh after; the monthly series 2019–2021 is continuous), which supports (but does not prove) that it is the same quantity (K23). **Annual pumping has grown strongly:** 29 GWh (2016), 107, 23, 71, 134, 78, 199 (2022), 319, **667 (2024)**, **836 GWh (2025, 24 % of hydro generation)**; 2026 so far (to 19 Oct): 386 GWh.
  * *Observation, 2026 only [data]:* by hour of the day (Greek local time) **87 % of the pumping energy falls between 10:00 and 16:00, 5 % in the morning, 7 % in the afternoon and 1 % at night (22:00–06:00)**, with the peak at 12:00–13:00 (18 % of the year's pumping in that one hour). In **January**, when solar output is low, **14 %** is still pumped at night; from February on the night share is 0–3 %. This is the profile of absorbing solar surplus, not of night pumping.
  * *Hypothesis [owner's, 20 Oct 2026; not tested]:* the growth follows the growth of photovoltaics: the need to reduce curtailment and to shift green energy in time, replacing the older way of running the pumps. *Anecdotal, unverified:* older colleagues said that the pumps worked at night to create demand so that the lignite units would not shut down.
  * *What is not known:* **how the pumping was distributed over the day before 2026** (no hourly history: the owner decided on 20 Oct 2026 not to store an hourly pumping profile for 2016–2025, D88), and what else contributes: the market rules (target model, Nov 2020), the hydrology of each year, the availability of the pumped-storage plants, and the batteries that arrived in 2025–26 (a possible reason why 2026 is below 2025). The numbers above show **when** the plants pump and **how much**, not **why**.
* **First readings (15 July of each year, indications only):** Ilarionas was at **15.2 % in 2026, the lowest of the eleven years** (next lowest 25.3 %); hydro generation that day was 17.8 GWh in 2026 against 12.0–13.7 GWh in 2020–2025; the total fill rate was 69.4 %, in the middle of the eleven years (56.7 % in 2025 to 78.7 % in 2023).

## 9. Clock-change days in the ADMIE files (diagnose run of 20 Oct 2026) [data]
*Observed in the real files of 2024, 2025 and 2026:*
* **SCADA** (`System_Production`, 222–251 rows × 27 columns): the label is in column 1, the **24 hour columns are 2–25**, and **column 26 is the daily total** (it equals the sum of the 24 hour columns, in MWh). The pipeline reads columns 2–25 (`SCADA_H0:SCADA_H1`). *The first version of the diagnose counted the total column as a 25th hour and reported a "50 % loss" even on ordinary days; its tests had no total column. Fixtures must reproduce the real structure of the files.*
* **23-hour days** (31 Mar 2024, 30 Mar 2025, 29 Mar 2026): still 24 hour columns, the **total equals their sum**, and the **last hour column is almost zero** (0, 1 and 2 MW of TOTAL HYDRO, against 268–700 MW at that hour on ordinary days).
* **25-hour days** (27 Oct 2024, 26 Oct 2025): still **24 hour columns**, but the **daily total is larger than their sum by 360 and 425 MWh (6.7 % and 6.4 % of the day)**: the 25th hour is only in the total. The evening peak of TOTAL HYDRO appears one column later than on an ordinary day (see the open point below).
* **ISP** (Kremasta row): since **1 Oct 2025** the file has **96 quarter-hour columns + a total column** (97 numeric cells); before that **48 half-hour columns + a total** (49; inferred from the counts 49, 47 and 51 on 24-, 23- and 25-hour days). On the **23-hour day of 29 Mar 2026: 92 quarters + the total (93 numeric cells)**; on the **25-hour day of 26 Oct 2025: 100 quarters + the total (101)**.
*Results of the second run (20 Oct 2026, with the days after each change):*
* **Both files are packed chronologically, not by clock hour.** On the three 23-hour days (2024, 2025, 2026) the **last** column is empty in every row (TOTAL HYDRO 0, 1, 2 MW; the system load row 0, 1, 2 MW): the 23 real hours occupy columns 0–22. The evening load peak sits in column 19 on all three 23-hour days, one column earlier than on the next (summer-time) day (20). On 25-hour days the evidence is weaker (a flat peak) and has to be confirmed on 25 Oct. *Consequence:* on a clock-change day the hours after 03:00 are one column off the clock, and a 24-column reading labels them H01–H24 as if nothing had happened.
* **The raw ISP is one hour ahead of SCADA on every ordinary day** (best shift +1, correlation 0.94–0.99), as the pipeline states (`CLOCK_NOTE`: ISP is on market (CET) time and is shifted +1 h by `isp_to_local`). This is expected, not an anomaly (the first version of the diagnose flagged it).
* **K04 is explained and proved.** `isp_to_local` takes H01 from `prev[23]`, hour 24 of the previous day's ISP. The 29 Mar file has 92 quarters, so the pipeline's 96-column reading puts the **daily-total column** at quarter 93, i.e. in hour 24, and the nan-mean of `[total, NaN, NaN, NaN]` is the total. In the stored data of **30 Mar 2026**, H01: `ISP_Kremasta` 1,855 MW (the rest of the day at most 317), `ISP_Ladonas` 1,140 (maximum 60), `ISP_Kastraki` 1,271, `ISP_PumpSfikia` 1,100; 10 of the 18 series are impossible. The values are the daily MWh of the 29 Mar file read as MW. Effect on `pump_daily.json`: the ISP pumping of 30 Mar is 2,167.5 MWh against 1,109 MWh (SCADA); the Sfikia/Thesavros split 932/177 is distorted; **SCADA-based quantities are unaffected**. (The raw correlation of the 29 Mar file itself is 0.04 for the same reason, 0.99 without hour 24.)
* **25-hour days:** the SCADA file has 24 hour columns and the daily total exceeds their sum by 360 and 425 MWh; the 25th hour of each row can therefore be recovered as `total − sum of the 24 columns` (shown for TOTAL HYDRO; to be checked for the unit rows). The ISP has 100 quarters + total; the pipeline reads 96.
*Design of the fix (a proposal, to be confirmed with the real files of 25 and 26 Oct; D90):* (1) take the number of intervals from the length of the day (92, 96 or 100 quarters; 23, 24 or 25 hours) and **never read the total column**; (2) map the chronological index to the clock hour (23-hour day: indices from 3 on belong to clock hour +1 and clock hour 03:00 is empty; 25-hour day: the repeated hour is combined and indices from 5 on belong to clock hour −1); (3) recover the 25th SCADA hour as `total − sum of the 24 columns`; (4) `isp_to_local`: H01 = the **last real market hour** of the previous day (index `n−1`), not index 23; (5) re-run 30 Mar and 25–26 Oct after the fix.
